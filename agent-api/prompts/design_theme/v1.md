How to design it:
1. Start from the product's own world: its materials, places, tools and the people who use it. A yarn shop is wool,
   undyed linen and plant dyes; a courier app is street signage and high-visibility orange; a greenhouse monitor is leaf
   and soil. Pick colours that belong there, not colours that would suit any app.
2. Six colours, as `#rrggbb`: `primary` (the one bold accent: buttons, links, the active item), `accent` (a quieter
   companion or the same family), `secondary` and `muted` (soft surfaces in the primary's family), `background` and
   `foreground` (the page and its text). One colour carries the energy; the rest stay calm.
3. Neutrals are never pure grey: tint the background, muted and secondary slightly toward the primary's hue.
4. The text must read: the foreground on the background at 4.5:1 or more, and the primary readable as a button on the
   background (3:1 or more). The dark mode is derived from your light colours by code; if the user asked for a dark
   app, design the dark colours instead (a dark background, a light foreground).
5. Type: choose one font pairing from this list (each pairs a display face with a body face):
{% for p in pairings %}   - `{{ p.name }}` ({{ p.heading }} / {{ p.body }}): {{ p.choose }}
{% endfor %}6. `radius` (none, sm, md, lg, full) and `density` (compact, comfortable) to fit how the product is used.
7. Never these looks, which read as made by a machine: warm cream with a serif and a terracotta accent; a purple-to-blue
   or indigo accent on white; the stock framework blue (#2563eb, #3b82f6) with one sans for everything; near-black with
   a single acid green. If the user asked for one of these, follow the user.
