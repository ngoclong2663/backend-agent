## Drafting order for dynamic apps

1. **FrontendSpec screens first**: design the user-facing screens using interactive sections (`DetailView`,
   `CardGrid`, `DataTable`, `ActionBar`, forms). Do NOT write any journeys (`"journeys": []`); tests are not required.
   Do not design authentication, sign-in, login, or user registration flows or forms (such as `auth.signin` or `auth.login`). Prototypes are open by default without login requirements.
2. **BackendSpec second**: entities, standard CRUD operations, plus domain operations (`type: "custom"`) for operations
   CRUD cannot fulfill (e.g. scheduling algorithms, statistics, batch operations). Every `operationId` referenced in FrontendSpec forms, actions, or data bindings MUST have a corresponding operation declared in BackendSpec (if a form has no backend operation, omit or set `operationId: null`). The generated Manage area
   automatically covers uncovered operations and administrative CRUD for entities, so do not design generic admin screens yourself.
{% endif %}

## How to build the pages

- **Content first.** List what the requirement actually gives: the offer, people, places, prices, opening hours, a
  process, a story, proof (testimonials, figures, clients), questions and policies. Give each the section that
  shows it best, from the whole catalogue {% if inline %}below{% else %}in `{{ sections_path }}`{% endif %} (Steps for a process, Team for people, PriceList
  for a menu or price list, Location for hours and address, Timeline for a story, Comparison for options, Statement
  for what the business stands for).
- **A section only exists when its content does.** Testimonials, client logos, Stats and Pricing only with real
  content from the requirement; Faq only when the requirement holds questions or policies a visitor needs (booking
  rules, delivery, payment), written from them; a CallToAction only for the requirement's main action. No section
  "because landing pages have one". What is missing goes to `assumptions` (e.g. "add testimonials when you have
  them") or `unresolved`, never into sample content.
- Every user-visible feature of the requirement maps to at least one page or section. Pages the requirement
  names come first, in its order; add only what a site of this kind clearly needs (and record it as an assumption).
- Use the built-in sections wherever they fit, with their typed `content`: these render without any model call. Use
  a custom component (in `components`, with a `propsSchema`) only for what no built-in section can show{% if mode == "dynamic" %}, typically a record view specific to this app{% endif %}.
- Navigation links (`layout.header.navigation`), footer links (`layout.footer.links`) and section CTAs (`page`) may
  only target pages without route parameters; detail pages are reached from a data section's record link.
- Icons: Features, Stats and Pricing items may take `"icon": "intent:<a concrete object, 1–3 words>"` where an icon
  helps a visitor scan (e.g. `intent:truck` for delivery, `intent:calendar` for booking, `intent:leaf` for organic).
  The site's icon set is chosen with the design and the intent is matched to a real icon. Not every item needs one,
  never on headings, and leave them out where numbers or plain text read better.
{% if design_path %}- **The home page is a pool.** Write every home section the content supports, each with a stable `id`; each
  direction below picks and orders its own home page from them. Mark the sections the requirement must have in
  `design.required`. Inner pages are shared by all directions.
{% else %}- Theme: use the brand colours and fonts the requirement names. Without them pick the `theme.style` preset that
  suits the kind of site (`minimal`, `editorial`, `playful`, `corporate`, `dashboard`) and leave the colours to
  the preset, unless the requirement describes a mood they should follow.
{% endif %}{% if photos_enabled %}- Images and Photos: an image of a real-world subject (food, places, people at work, products, nature, culture, events) is `source: "photo"` with `kind: "image"` and a `prompt` that names the subject, the setting, the light and the framing, e.g. 'a steaming bowl of pho on a street stall table, morning light, close-up, shallow depth of field'. For collections and seed records with an image field, set the field to `asset:<entity>-<n>` and declare that asset with `source: "photo"`, a prompt describing that record (e.g. the dish's name and look), the collection's aspect ratio, and an alt text. An illustration or the logo may be a photo too (keep its `kind`: `illustration`, `logo`); patterns and icons never are. Never write image URLs. At most {{ max_photos }} photos (records and pages share this cap); give them to the images that matter most (hero, key records). Images beyond the cap stay `"source": "generate"`.
{% endif %}{% if videos_enabled %}- Videos: only when the requirement has something people **watch** (talks, lessons, demos, films, events with
  recordings) or a first screen that suits motion. A video is an `assets` entry with `"kind": "video"`,
  `"source": "search"`, a short `query` of what it shows (the subject, the setting: 'a potter centering clay on the
  wheel'), a `place` (`watch` for a video people play, `background` for a muted loop behind the hero's text) and an
  `alt`. Show watched videos with the `Video` section (`video`, or `items` of two to six); put a `background` video
  in the hero's `backgroundVideo` (with the `banner` or `stage` layout) only when the site would gain a moving first
  screen. Never write video URLs, ids or iframes; code finds each video and credits it. At most four videos per site.
{% endif %}{% if not photos_enabled %}- Images: one `assets` entry per image (the hero, galleries, work samples, section backgrounds, the logo), each
  with a real `alt` text, and never a placeholder: every image is drawn. Give it `"source": "generate"`, a `kind`
  (`illustration`, `image`, `pattern` or `logo`) and a `prompt` that says what to draw for this site: a concrete
  scene or an abstract composition, without words in it. Drawings are flat vector art in the site's theme colours;
  when the requirement asks for photography, describe an editorial illustration of the same subject and record that
  as an assumption (the user can upload photos later). At most 12 drawn images per site.

## Design: three directions

{% if inline %}The design facts below give{% else %}`{{ design_path }}` has the design facts:{% endif %} the category default for this requirement (the look to move away from),
the hero layouts, section forms, fonts and key visuals the renderer draws, and a variation number. Write three
**direction cards** in the notes, made together and deliberately far apart, each fitting the requirement:

- `concept`: one sentence, an idea taken from the product, not adjectives: a material in its setting, a scene, a
  character, a design tradition, another medium, or one convention of the category broken on purpose (`engine`
  names which: `material`, `scene`, `character`, `tradition`, `medium`, `convention`). `evidence` says which words
  of the requirement it comes from.
- `tone`: `energy`, `finish`, `density`, `weight`, `seriousness`, each 1–5; it sets colour area, type ratio,
  radius, surfaces and heading weight for you.
- `heroLayout`, `keyVisual`, `fonts` (`heading`, `body`, bundled only), `colors` (`primary`, `background`, optional
  `foreground`, `muted`, `accent`, and `source`: where the colour comes from in the product or brand, never "the
  concept's material"), `sectionForms` (optional), `rhythm`.
- `home`: the ids of the pool's sections in this direction's order (a subset that keeps every required section).
- `givesUp`: what this direction sacrifices; `fit`: 1, 2, 3 for how well it fits the requirement (1 best).
- Optional, otherwise taken from the tone: `icons` (`{"set": "lucide" | "phosphor" | "none", "weight": "thin" |
  "light" | "regular" | "bold" | "fill" | "duotone", "treatment": "plain" | "tile"}`; `none` for raw or editorial
  directions), `motion` (a string, the feel: `none`, `click`, `slide`, `flow`, `drop`, `bounce`, `float`, `cut`) and
  `signature` (a string beside it, one stronger moment: `countUp` for figures, `headlineRise`, `imageUnmask`,
  `indicator`), e.g. `"motion": "flow", "signature": "headlineRise"`.

The difference test runs on them: any two directions share at most one of hero layout, heading font, hue family
(within 30°) and key visual; the three hero layouts all differ; at least one home page leaves the category's usual
order; any two home pages differ in their first three sections. Each direction its own hue family; never "one light, one dark,
one warm". If the requirement names brand colours or fonts, every card uses them (and record them, see Notes).
Section variants beyond the cards are optional: `background` (`muted`, `primary`) sets off a section, `cardStyle`
changes the cards of a list section; use them sparingly.
